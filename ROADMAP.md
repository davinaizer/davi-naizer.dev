# Roadmap

## Purpose

This roadmap defines the delivery sequence for the product described in `PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS.md`.

It organises work into small, complete outcomes without prescribing implementation details.

The roadmap must remain subordinate to `PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS.md`. Changes should follow demonstrated product or engineering needs rather than speculative features.

## Roadmap principles

A roadmap defines:

- outcomes;
- sequencing; and
- priorities.

It does not define:

- implementation;
- architecture;
- tasks; or
- estimates.

Those concerns belong elsewhere.

The roadmap also follows these product principles:

- Build a production-quality React and TypeScript application, not primarily a portfolio or marketing website.
- Preserve learning through implementation as a product objective.
- Deliver small, useful increments while keeping completed work deployable.
- Treat accessibility, responsive behaviour, testing, maintainability, and performance as product concerns.
- Prefer evidence-backed scope over feature accumulation.
- Keep architecture and tooling proportional to current requirements.

The next milestone must not begin until the current milestone's completion conditions are met.

## Milestone 1: Application foundation

### Outcome

Establish a deployable application foundation that supports deliberate React, TypeScript, CSS, accessibility, testing, and frontend engineering practice.

### Depends on

None.

### Product objectives

- Maintain frontend engineering fluency.
- Demonstrate engineering judgement.

### Scope

- Foundation only; no professional content yet
- React and strict TypeScript application foundation
- Application shell, route structure, shared layout, and navigation framework
- Content and presentation structure suitable for progressive enhancement
- Baseline styling and responsive behaviour
- Accessibility foundations
- Proportional automated quality checks and tests
- Build and deployment foundation

### Completion conditions

- The application builds and can be deployed.
- The initial structure is understandable and maintainable.
- Strict TypeScript and relevant quality checks pass.
- Accessibility and responsive behaviour have an explicit baseline.
- No speculative architecture or unnecessary dependency has been introduced.

### Depends on

Milestone 1.

### Product objectives

- Maintain frontend engineering fluency.
- Consolidate professional knowledge.
- Demonstrate engineering judgement.

### Scope

- Home
- Experience
- Resume
- Contact

### Completion conditions

## Milestone 5: Evidence-driven evolution

### Outcome

Evolve the product sustainably after the MVP using demonstrated needs rather than a speculative feature backlog.

### Depends on

Milestone 4.

### Product objectives

- Improve interview readiness.
- Maintain frontend engineering fluency.
- Consolidate professional knowledge.
- Demonstrate engineering judgement.

### Scope

Consider changes only when supported by:

- interview feedback;
- recruiter or hiring-manager conversations;
- implementation experience;
- repeated maintenance pain;
- demonstrated engineering needs; or
- evidence that existing content is difficult to reuse or understand.

### Completion conditions

This milestone remains ongoing. Each proposed change must:

- support at least one product objective;
- solve a demonstrated current problem;
- be deliverable as a small, complete increment;
- preserve maintainability and deployability; and
- avoid the product non-goals in `PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS.md`.

## MVP boundary

Milestones 2 and 3 define the complete first-public-release scope. No additional product area is part of the MVP.

The roadmap does not include a blog, CMS, knowledge-management system, SaaS functionality, social features, AI experimentation, or showcase-driven visual effects.

## Roadmap maintenance

- Change milestone scope only when product evidence justifies it.
- Keep task-level execution elsewhere.
- Keep architecture elsewhere.
- Keep this document focused on milestone outcomes.
